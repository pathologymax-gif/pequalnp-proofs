# Two unrestricted gate-count bounds for the order-three projective plane

On the 52 incidence and 234 quadrangle inputs in `L-meta-2063`, a monotone binary circuit needs **at least nine AND gates and at least 21 OR gates**, even with arbitrary fanout. These arguments establish a total lower bound of 30. They do not establish the project's 64-gate lower bound or the smaller-circuit conclusion.

Each positive input is an incidence edge in the bipartite graph on thirteen points and thirteen lines. It has exactly two true inputs. Each negative input contains four points of a quadrangle and the three lines missing those points. These seven vertices form a maximal independent set. The verifier enumerates all 234 such sets from the Singer coordinates. A point appears in 72 negative inputs and a line in 54. Two distinct raw variables appear together in at most 24.

## Nine AND gates with arbitrary ORs

For each negative input q, form a set of binary signatures with one bit per AND. On an AND whose operands differ at q, the corresponding bit must select the false operand. If the operand values agree, either choice is allowed. This set is nonempty. The signature sets of any two distinct negatives q,r are disjoint. To see this, use an incidence edge contained in their union. That incidence is positive, but neither q nor r contains both of its vertices. No raw variable is therefore true on the incidence and false on both q and r. A common signature, if one existed, would let us trace that pattern from the output to a raw variable. At OR, select an operand true on the incidence. At AND, both operands are true on the incidence, and the shared bit selects an operand false on both q and r. All three properties persist.

Since 234 exceeds 128, seven ANDs are impossible. Suppose there are eight. The first AND is preceded only by ORs, so its operands are unions of raw variable columns A and B. After fixing the first signature bit there are only 128 suffixes. Every negative except those forced to the other bit must occupy at least one suffix. Hence both the number of rows in A excluding B and in B excluding A must be at least 106. It follows that each union separately must hit between 106 and 128 negatives. Every triple of distinct raw variables hits more than 128. Exactly 130 two-variable subsets qualify, and all of them hit 126 rows. Distinct eligible unions overlap on at least 36 rows. The greatest possible minimum of the two exclusive counts is consequently 90.

## Twenty-one OR gates with arbitrary ANDs

Let m be the number of ORs. Fix a negative input q. Starting at the output, retain both inputs of each zero-valued OR and one zero-valued input at each zero-valued AND. A forward positive input accepted by a selected wire is still accepted by some retained operand. The final raw wires jointly cover all 52 positives and are zero at q. Every one of the nineteen vertices outside q is required. In fact each has a neighbor in q, and their positive incidence can be covered only by the outside vertex. This reverse traversal of a shared circuit can increase the number of distinct retained wires by at most one per selected OR. Hence at least eighteen OR gates are zero on q. Summing over all q, the total number of true OR-gate/negative-input incidences is at most 234(m−18).

The following second traversal keeps track of positive coverage. Flatten AND-only paths so each operand of an OR is a conjunction of raw inputs and previously computed OR signals. At the output and within each operand, if the conjunction contains any previous OR signal, choose one; it covers every positive the conjunction covers. Otherwise retain the whole raw conjunction as a leaf. At each retained OR, visit both operands. Visiting each distinct OR at most once produces a connected graph with at most m binary branching vertices and thus at most m+1 distinct terminal leaves. Those leaves jointly cover all 52 incidence edges. If the entire output were just a raw conjunction, it could cover at most four positives and would already be impossible. Thus each leaf is a raw-conjunction operand of a counted OR.

Any raw conjunction of two or more distinct variables is true on at most one positive edge. A conjunction supported on one variable is true on exactly four. Upon identifying equal raw conjunctions, let d be the number of distinct singleton leaves. If m≤20, then 52 ≤ m+1+3d, giving d≥11. Charge each singleton to one occurrence at an OR where it is an operand. At this gate, every negative that contains the singleton variable makes the gate true. When two singletons are charged to the same gate, their two high-row sets overlap in at most 24 positions. Every singleton has at least 54 high rows and each gate receives at most two charges. The total number of true OR-gate/negative-input incidences is therefore at least 54d−24⌊d/2⌋ ≥ 474. The preceding upper bound is at most 468. This excludes twenty ORs.
