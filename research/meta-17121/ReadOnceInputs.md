# Bounding the number of primary inputs read more than once

For the four G11 projections, every minimum circuit of size at most 18 can be represented by an equally small circuit which reads at least 3, 3, 4 and 3 distinct primary inputs more than once, respectively. Combined with the independently verified two-shared-wire requirement, this proves unrestricted lower bounds of **14, 14, 15 and 14 gates**. Here is the normalization, the restriction on a single reader, and the finite calculation behind these numbers. A separate SAT verification can refine the size bound once these inputs are fixed.

## Extracting the final AND gates

Call a signal *clean* if it is zero on every maximal-independent-set test. Call a signal *dirty* if it is one on at least one such test. Every nonzero primary is dirty. Remove the zero primary by folding gates that read it, remove unreachable gates, and choose a minimum circuit computing the target. Every signal of this minimum circuit is one on some positive edge, since otherwise replacing it by zero preserves all positive and all negative tests and permits removal of a gate.

Every clean AND can be transferred to a final disjunction without increasing total size. Work in topological order and keep a residual circuit R. Take the first remaining clean AND h with a path to the residual output. Replace every residual use of h by zero, fold constants, and include h among the final disjuncts. Keep the live computation of h. When h is zero the residual is unchanged. When h is one the sample is positive, and the newly included h now ensures output one. At least one direct reader folds away to pay for including this extra disjunct, unless h already is the residual output, in which case it is the final term and no extra disjunction is needed for replacing that output. Frozen earlier terms contain no reader of h. Because h is zero on every negative, the exact values of all surviving residual signals on every negative are unchanged. A clean OR has only clean operands. Consequently a residual with no more clean ANDs cannot have a nonzero clean output: tracing backwards from such an OR on a positive edge would reach a clean AND or a clean primary. The latter is impossible. Remove a residual zero by constant folding.

The result is the OR of t clean AND products of raw inputs or dirty intermediates. The dirty part can have arbitrary fan-out and arbitrary AND/OR alternation. All its gates precede the clean products. The final disjunction can be arranged into any binary tree with t−1 gates. If d gates remain in the dirty part, a minimum has exactly d+2t−1 gates in this form. An unnecessarily retained gate, or a product lacking a private positive edge, could be removed to obtain a smaller circuit.

## Following a primary input with one reader

Let R consist of the nonzero primary inputs having at least two direct readers in the normalized minimum. Initialize A to R. The aim is to bound which other variables can enter a dirty intermediate. Two raw inputs cannot share a gate that is the only reader for either of them. At an AND such a variable loses another incident edge, since the minimum degree is two. At an OR the other input is one on a maximal independent set omitting this variable. Adding the variable to that set changes the required output to one but leaves the OR unchanged. The verifier checks the needed negative set exists for every ordered pair of distinct raw variables.

Suppose z has just one reader and its partner is a dirty signal h over variables already in A. The function on every subset of vertices is determined by monotonicity from its values on edges and maximal independent sets.

* At an AND reader, h is one on every singleton neighbor of z. Otherwise, turning on z there cannot change the output. Thus every neighbor of z belongs to A. For the reader to be dirty, some maximal independent M containing z also makes h one. Such M contains none of the neighbors of z. Hence A must contain at least one additional vertex.
* At an OR reader, h is zero on every maximal independent set omitting z. Adding z to such a set changes the output and would leave a reader already equal to one unchanged. Since h is dirty, some maximal independent M containing z makes h one. Its pattern M∩A cannot be contained in any maximal independent set omitting z, by monotonicity. Finally the reader must improve on raw z on some positive edge avoiding z; otherwise replacing it by z would preserve the target and save the reader. That edge lies in A. Indeed h cannot accept a singleton variable, since every raw vertex distinct from z occurs in a maximal independent set missing z. It cannot accept the empty input either.

Add every z satisfying one of the two necessary conditions and iterate until A stops growing. Induction in the dirty circuit's evaluation order shows that A includes the support of every dirty intermediate. A variable with one reader may instead enter only a terminal product. No terminal product can accept an edge disjoint from A: a dirty intermediate over A is zero there, and two raw inputs from outside A cannot have their sole reader at the same gate. Therefore A must be a vertex cover.

`python read_once_closure.py` enumerates repeated-input sets of each cardinality, works to a fixed point, and tests the cover condition. Among the 45 pairs in each projection none survives. Among the 120 triples in each projection there remain 1, 2, 0 and 12 possible sets, respectively. Surviving means that this test alone does not exclude the set.

## Turning readers into gates

If there are s binary gates, there are 2s operand occurrences. Every one of the s−1 internal gate outputs preceding the final output must have at least one reader. The [one-shared-gate lower bound](OneSharedGate.md) shows that whenever s≤18, two distinct intermediates each have at least one extra reader. This leaves at most s−1 primary occurrences. All ten nonzero primaries are essential, and those in R each need an additional occurrence. Hence s≥11+|R|. The four stated lower bounds follow. Circuits with more than 18 gates automatically meet them.
